# Co-Founder Mistakes That Kill Companies & How To Avoid Them

Video Link: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dlfjs_eEEzs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dlfjs_eEEzs)

* **Write it down:** It is extremely hard to deal with bad situations after the fact without written agreements. Write down who gets what percentage of the company and whether you have founder vesting early on when everything is going well.
* **Prioritize relationship over skill match:** Do not look for a co-founder based solely on a skill match. Almost everything you learn is on the job. Work with someone you already know and have pressure-tested (e.g., someone you have had a fight with before).
* **Take arguments seriously:** Co-founder disputes are more likely to kill a company than anything else.
* **Manage conflicts tactically:**
* **Pause:** You don't always have to come to an immediate resolution. If you aren't engaging productively, pause and don't keep the fight going.
* **Understand stress responses:** Learn if your co-founder deals with stress by attacking or retreating so you can better interpret their actions.


* **Do not avoid conflict:** Choosing a co-founder with whom you cannot share honest disagreements leads to conflict avoidance or fighting dirty. If communication breaks down to the point of not speaking for long periods, breaking up becomes inevitable.
* **Execute inevitable breakups effectively:** If a breakup is necessary, the CEO's job is no longer to repair the relationship but to separate in the least destructive way. Delaying increases the risk of litigation and cap table issues from vested stock.
* **Structure equity to survive battles:**
* Keep equity as close to equal as possible to avoid drama over who owns the company.
* Avoid straight 50/50 deadlocks. Give the CEO one extra share to serve as a tiebreaker vote in the event of a deadlock, and agree to this in writing early.


* **Find the co-founder before the idea:** Figure out the co-founder first, then come up with the idea and fundraise together to build collective ownership. Adding a co-founder later often makes them feel it is not their company, which hurts retention during hard times.
* **Front-load the effort:** Setting up the co-founder relationship is one of the most important things in the beginning of a company. Invest heavily in the relationship early; do not slack off or expect you can easily quit and start over if things fail.
