# Growth for Startups

### Product-Market Fit (PMF) and Retention
* Do not work on growth before achieving product-market fit and good retention.
* Launching does not automatically bring users; you must actively reach them.
* Do things that don't scale in the early days. Founders must do unconventional, non-scalable tasks to acquire early customers and learn their exact pain points.
* The best, most unbiased way to measure PMF is through repeat usage (retention data), not surveys or Net Promoter Scores (NPS).
* To properly measure retention:
  * Identify the specific metric that represents the true value of your product.
  * Determine the expected frequency of that action for a typical user.
  * Track the percentage of users returning to perform that core action over specific time windows. A curve that eventually flattens out indicates PMF. A curve dropping to zero indicates a bad product.
* Bad metrics for measuring PMF: Registered users, raw visitors, general conversion rates, and willingness to use a paid product for free.

### Growth Channels and Tactics (Post-PMF)
* Scale growth through two main avenues once PMF is established: Product Growth (Conversion Rate Optimization) and external Growth Channels. Most companies scale using only one or two channels.
* **Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO):** Put a metric on every step of your product experience to measure the funnel. Find where users drop off and optimize. Key areas to optimize include internationalization, authentication, onboarding, and purchase conversion flows.
* **Search / SEO:** Essential for products addressing rare behaviors (infrequent actions people actively search for). SEO requires optimization of on-page elements (readable text, strategic keywords based on volume) and off-page elements (domain authority, inbound links).
* **Referrals and Virality:** Vital if your product gets better with more users or has high word-of-mouth potential. Offer financial incentives and optimize the referral invite funnel: use a recognized sender name, state a clear value proposition, include a deadline, and use a clear call-to-action.
* **Direct Sales:** If your potential customer base is relatively finite, list the decision-makers and contact them directly rather than building complex marketing campaigns.
* **Paid Growth (Online Ads):** Never run paid marketing unless the product generates revenue. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) must be definitively lower than the expected user revenue. Establish early indicators (1–3 months) to ensure CAC profitability.

### Decision Making at Scale
* Build a culture of experimentation using unbiased data rather than relying on product instinct or the loudest voice in the room. 
* Use A/B testing to measure the exact impact of new designs or features by running parallel versions simultaneously.
* Before launching an experiment, use an A/B testing calculator to determine if you have enough traffic to make the test mathematically worthwhile.
