# How Much Equity to Give Your Cofounder - Michael Seibel

[Video Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NhEBVPlJs4)

* Equity splits dictate the motivation of your co-founders to stick with the company through the years required to build it.
* Co-founders often underestimate the long-term time commitment required at the start; it is the CEO's job to consider what they would want to stay motivated long-term.
* Prioritize an equity split that maximizes team motivation over simply accepting whatever split was negotiated.
* Your primary safety mechanism when giving equity is vesting and a cliff.
* Standard terms include four-year vesting (working four years to earn the full equity stake) and a one-year cliff (receiving nothing if leaving or fired within the first year).
* This structure acts as a hedge: if you make an incorrect decision about a co-founder, you can correct it within the first year without causing long-term harm to the company.
* Because this hedge exists, it is generally better to be more generous with equity rather than less.
* Generous equity ensures you do not have to manually motivate your co-founders every day; their stake drives them to work hard and act like true owners, not just employees, especially during difficult times.
* Equal equity splits are a good rule of thumb, though they cannot be applied in every situation.
* If you do not feel a teammate is worth a generous equity grant, you should reconsider making them a co-founder entirely.
