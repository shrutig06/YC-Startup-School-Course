# How To Talk To Users | Startup School

[Watch the video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z1iF1c8w5Lg)

## Why Talk to Users
* Learn directly from users throughout the life of the company.
* Users and customers will keep you honest; they are the only stakeholders paying you.
* Build personal connections; do not hide behind "do not reply" emails.

## Who to Talk to and How to Find Them
* **Your Network:** Likely to respond, but less honest to avoid offending you.
* **Co-workers/Former Co-workers:** Great source if they know the topic well.
* **Strangers:** Find early users via LinkedIn, Reddit, forums, Slack/Discord communities, and in-person events. 
* **Outreach Strategy:** Introduce yourself briefly, mention the project without too much detail, and ask for a 20-minute call.

## How to Interview Potential Customers
* Conduct interviews over video calls, phone calls, or in person (a 5-minute conversation provides more value than thousands of survey responses).
* Build rapport so the interviewee trusts you and feels comfortable.
* **Do not introduce your idea** until the end, or not at all, to avoid biasing their answers.
* Your role is to listen, not to talk.
* Ask open-ended follow-up questions (e.g., "Tell me about that", "What do you mean by that?").
* Take notes if you are not recording.

## Questions to Ask
* Tell me how you do [X] today?
* What is the hardest thing you are doing about [X]?
* Why is it hard?
* How often do you have to do [X]?
* Why is it important for your company to do [X]? (Dive deep to understand the motivation).
* What do you do to solve this problem for yourself today?
* *Tip:* Have them screen-share or watch them complete the task to deeply understand their actual behavior, not just what they say.

## Questions NOT to Ask
* **"Will you use our product?"** (They will say yes, but it means nothing).
* **"Which features would make product X better?"** (Their job is to explain the problem; your job is to identify features).
* **Yes and no questions** (You want them to explain and give concrete examples).
* **"How would a better product look to you?"** (They are not product developers).
* **Asking two questions at the same time** (It confuses their answers).

## Focus on Problems, Not Features
* Users generally have good problems but bad solutions.
* Users have no incentive to say no to additional features; you must be in charge of prioritization.

## Next Steps and MVP Testing
* Use sticky notes to bucket learnings by problem and identify what matters most.
* Write down conclusions, create a hypothesis for a solution, and design a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) quickly.
* **Evaluate if the problem is valuable:**
    * Are people paying money for other solutions in the space today?
    * Do they already have solutions they are happy with?
    * How easy is it to sell to this audience? 
* **Testing Prototypes:**
    * Do not tell them what to do; just watch them play around with it.
    * Give them a specific goal, but do not tell them exactly how to solve each screen.
    * Have them speak their mind and describe what they are thinking at each step.

## Keeping Users Involved
* Create a Slack or WhatsApp group for early customers.
* Make them feel special with exclusive access to a future world-changing product.
* Show product progression and react fast to their feedback to build trust.
* Enable them to connect with other people doing the same thing.
