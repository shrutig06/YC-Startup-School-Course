# How to Apply And Succeed at Y Combinator

[Video link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B5tU2447OK8)

## Why Apply to YC
*   **Clarifies Thinking:** Answering application questions forces you to organize your startup idea and identify edge cases.
*   **High ROI:** The application is intentionally fast and simple to complete, meaning the time cost is low compared to the massive potential upside.
*   **Creates Luck:** Putting yourself in situations where you face rejection increases your surface area for luck. Applying is a low-risk way to build this skill.

## Invalid Reasons Not to Apply
*   **"I am too early":** There is no such thing. Startups get in before writing code, before founders quit their jobs, and many pivot entirely after acceptance.
*   **"I am too far along":** Companies with $500K ARR or over $1M raised still get accepted. If you lack product-market fit, YC can help.
*   **"Free videos are enough":** Being in a YC batch provides 1-on-1 access to partners, confidential advice never shared online, and proprietary internal tools.
*   **"Gatekeepers discourage it":** If an investor discourages you from applying but refuses to invest on clear terms themselves, challenge them.
*   **"My location/market is different":** YC funds companies in every vertical and from every country.
*   **"I was rejected before":** Applying multiple times is viewed as a positive indicator of persistence. Many accepted founders applied multiple times.

## Writing a Successful Application
*   **Complete Everything:** Fill out the entire application, including founder biographies. Incomplete sections signal a lack of seriousness.
*   **Execute the Basics:** Pay attention to grammar, punctuation, and capitalization. 
*   **Follow Directions Exactly:** Follow all instructions for the founder video. YC uses this to test if founders are detail-oriented.
*   **Be Clear and Concise:** Answer questions directly without writing a novel. Avoid jargon that obscures what you actually do.
    *   *Example:* GitLab's successful application clearly and concretely stated what it was, what it did, and exactly who used it in just a few straightforward sentences. 
*   **Tell a Cohesive Story:** The reader should easily understand who the founders are, how they met, what is built today, and what evidence exists that users want it.
*   **Be Honest:** Intentional misrepresentation (e.g., framing annual revenue as monthly) results in automatic disqualification. 
*   **Back Up Big Claims:** Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence. 
*   **Show Seriousness:** Actions like working on the startup full-time or demonstrating progress between applications prove commitment.

## Improving Interview Odds
*   **Technical Talent:** Having a founding team with the technical skill to be hired at a top YC company increases your odds of getting an interview by roughly 5x. 
*   **Avoid Tar Pit Ideas:** Non-technical teams working on unlaunched, highly competitive "tar pit" ideas have the lowest odds of acceptance.
*   **Find Co-Founders:** Use YC Co-Founder Matching to add technical talent to your team.
*   **Ignore Scammers:** YC is designed to fund complete strangers based on a web form. Warm intros, pitch decks, and giving away equity to "connectors" will not help you get in.

## Acing the Interview
*   **The Format:** A 10-minute Zoom call with 2-4 YC partners. All founders must be present. Partners will actively read your application during the call.
*   **Expect Contextual Questions:** Questions are basic but tailored. Pre-launch companies will be asked when they plan to launch; regulated companies will be asked about regulatory strategy.
*   **It is Not Adversarial:** The interviewers are assessing if they want to work with you. Being defensive or argumentative will backfire.
*   **Do Not Recite:** Avoid delivering memorized speeches or robotic answers. Listen to the actual question being asked.
*   **Be Authentic:** Be credible, honest, and self-aware about your startup's real risks. Do not act like you are pitching on a reality show.
*   **Address Past Feedback:** If you were rejected previously, internalizing and fixing the actionable feedback provided in your rejection email heavily weighs in your favor in future applications.
