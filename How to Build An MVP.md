# How to Build An MVP

[How to Build An MVP](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QRZ_l7cVzzU)

## Core Concept of an MVP
* An MVP (Minimum Viable Product) must be ridiculously simple.
* It is the very first version given to initial target users to test if it delivers any value.
* It is not special; it is only a starting point and a base for iteration.

## Goals of a Pre-Launch Startup
* **Launch quickly:** Launch something bad, quickly.
* **Get initial customers:** Get anyone to interact with and use the product.
* **Talk to users:** Gather feedback on the initial product, regardless of how incomplete it is.
* **Iterate:** Continually improve the solution until the problem is solved.
* **Mindset:** Hold the problem and the customer tightly, but hold the solution loosely.

## Building a Lean MVP
* **Build it fast:** Construction should take weeks, not months. A landing page and a spreadsheet can suffice.
* **Extremely limited functionality:** Condense features to a simple set. Focus on a small initial user base and their highest-order problems, ignoring the rest until later.
* *Example:* Airbnb's MVP lacked a map view and digital payments (users exchanged money in person).

## Heavy MVPs (Hard Tech, Biotech, Regulated Industries)
* Launching quickly is difficult due to regulations or complex technology.
* Start with a simple website built in days that explains the product's function to serve as a reference during customer conversations.

## The Reality of Launching
* Launches are not special; people do not remember the launch days of successful companies.
* Distinguish between a "press launch" (getting buzz) and a "get-any-customers launch."
* Delay the press launch and prioritize getting any customers as soon as possible.
* True customer learning requires putting a product in front of them to see if it solves their problem.

## Hacks for Quick MVP Building
* **Timebox your spec:** Set a strict deadline (e.g., three weeks) and include only what can be built within that time.
* **Write your spec down:** Document the exact feature list before launch to prevent scope creep and accidental plan changes.
* **Cut your spec:** If the deadline is at risk, cut features that are not absolutely critical to solving the user's problem.
* **Do not fall in love with your MVP:** The product and solution will inevitably change based on user feedback.
