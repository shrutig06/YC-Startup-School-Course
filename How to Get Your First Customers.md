# How to Get Your First Customers

[Video Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hyYCn_kAngI)

## Mindset & Founder Responsibilities

* Do things that don't scale: manually recruit customers instead of relying on ads or writing code.
* Founders must do sales themselves; talking to customers and sales are the same thing.
* Do not hire a sales team or outsource until you know how to sell and know what good looks like.

## The Sales Funnel & Tracking

* **Stages:** Prospecting -> Outreach -> Demo/Meeting -> Talk Pricing -> Close -> Onboard.
* Onboarding is mandatory; skipping it leads to churn because early products are hard to use.
* Work backwards from your goal by tracking conversion rates at every step (opens, responses, demos, closes).
* Outbound sales is a numbers game. You must send a high volume to find the rare early adopters.
* Never conclude sales doesn't work just because a small batch of emails yielded zero customers.

## Targeting & Prioritization

* Your first customers should be your easiest. Do not chase every lead.
* Keep a large pipeline, ask qualifying questions early, and prioritize leads most likely to close.
* Avoid slow-moving leads and let customers go if they drag the process out.
* Use your personal network to find early users.
* Sell to startups; they are the easiest B2B targets due to short decision-making lines and zero bureaucracy.

## Pricing

* Charge for your product. Customers paying money proves you provide real value.
* If a prospect refuses to pay, they are a bad fit—move on immediately.
* Do not offer B2B free trials. Use a money-back guarantee (30-60 days) or a month-to-month opt-out instead.
* Continually increase your price until customers complain but still pay.

## Writing Great Sales Emails

* Keep it strictly short (6 to 8 sentences max).
* Use plain, clear language. Address their specific problem with zero jargon or buzzwords.
* Use plain text formatting only (no HTML).
* Embed social proof (e.g., past impressive companies, YC batch) to establish authority. Show, don't tell.
* Include a link to a simple website containing screenshots and bullet points (avoid fancy paid graphics).
* End with a single, clear call to action (ask for a call, meeting, or self-serve signup).
