# Kevin Hale - How to Work Together
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=30a5yFBd7Fo)

*   **Everyone fights:** Conflict is inevitable and usually centers around the same core issues. Make a plan for dealing with it before it happens.
*   **Four behaviors to avoid (The Four Horsemen):**
    *   **Criticism:** Bringing in outside issues instead of addressing the specific issue at hand.
    *   **Contempt:** Intention to insult or make things personal.
    *   **Defensiveness:** Not owning responsibility or refusing to admit there is a problem.
    *   **Stonewalling:** Walking away, shutting down communication, or refusing to engage.
*   **Four strategies to protect against destructive fighting:**
    *   **1. Divide and conquer (Protects against defensiveness):**
        *   Delegate responsibilities and ownership early in the startup.
        *   Determine what constitutes success (no interference needed) and failure (triggers hard conversations and interference).
        *   Decide ahead of time how conflicts are resolved (ultimately, the CEO or the board has the final say).
    *   **2. Know your attachment style (Protects against stonewalling):**
        *   *Secure:* Comfortable relying on others and being vulnerable.
        *   *Anxious:* Needs constant validation and clings to people.
        *   *Avoidant:* Needs space to process and wants to run away from conflict.
        *   Understand your co-founder's style. Anxious and avoidant types often pair up and must accommodate each other's needs for processing conflict (providing space vs. providing validation).
    *   **3. Document a process (Protects against criticism):**
        *   Create a framework for handling disagreements while emotionally sober.
        *   Document disagreements transparently: list the options, who makes the decision, the final decision, the date, and the rationale.
        *   Agree ahead of time on a deadlock resolution (e.g., take a timeout, flip a coin).
    *   **4. Use nonviolent communication (Protects against contempt):**
        *   *Structure:* "When [observation], I feel [emotion] because I'm needing some [universal need], would you be able to [request]?"
        *   *Observation vs. Evaluation:* Anchor feedback to concrete, irrefutable facts, not opinions or generalizations (e.g., "You arrived 10 minutes late" instead of "You're always late").
        *   *Emotions vs. Thoughts:* State true feelings (e.g., "I feel frustrated") rather than thoughts disguised as feelings ("I feel that you aren't taking this seriously"). Unpack evaluative emotions to find the root feeling (e.g., feeling "blamed" is actually feeling "scared").
        *   *Universal Need vs. Strategy:* Connect the emotion to a truly universal need (e.g., "I need transparency"), rather than a specific strategy or person (e.g., "I need you to copy me on every email").
        *   *Request vs. Demand:* Offer an invitation for specific, desired behavior. Say what you *want*, not what you *don't want* (e.g., "I request you ask two probing questions before sharing a conclusion").
*   **Pay down emotional debt daily:**
    *   Address small issues immediately and respectfully so they do not grow into massive problems.
*   **Practice Level 3 (Relational) Conversations:**
    *   Engage in deep-dives about immediate, relational issues between team members.
    *   *Goals:* Align on short-term goals and ensure the right metrics are used.
    *   *Roles:* Clarify who is responsible for what and agree on the division of labor.
    *   *Performance:* Regularly check workload distribution, motivation levels, and feedback mechanisms.
