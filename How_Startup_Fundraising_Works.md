# How Startup Fundraising Works

[Video Link](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zBUhQPPS9AY)

## Key Concepts and Actionable Lessons

*   **Myth 1: Raising money is glamorous**
    *   Reality: Startup fundraising is a grind consisting of quiet, one-on-one meetings and coffee chats. 
    *   Pitch competitions and TV shows are marketing events, not actual fundraising.

*   **Myth 2: You need to raise money before you can start working on your startup**
    *   Reality: Build the first version of the product and get some users first.
    *   Investors want to jump on trains that are in motion. Having a working product and early users provides immediate leverage.

*   **Myth 3: Your startup needs to be impressive to raise money**
    *   Reality: You do not need to impress investors; you need to convince them. Most great startups seem terrible at first.
    *   Actionable Step: Do not use magic words or over-the-top pitches. Show the product, explain the value it creates for users, and use plain language to explain how there is a 1% chance it can get huge.

*   **Myth 4: Raising money is complicated, slow, and expensive**
    *   Reality: While late-stage rounds are massive and complex, typical seed rounds are small, fast, and cheap.
    *   Actionable Step: Use the YC SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity). It is a simple, free 5-page document that requires no lawyers and allows you to close investments quickly using just two terms: the investment amount and the valuation cap.

*   **Myth 5: Raising money means losing control of your company**
    *   Reality: Seed rounds today give founders more control than ever. 
    *   Raising on SAFEs means giving up no board seats, creating no immediate shareholders, and granting no information rights.
    *   Bootstrapping forever stretches the pain of fundraising across the life of the company; taking the pain of fundraising upfront allows you to run the company how you want and answer only to customers.

*   **Myth 6: You need a fancy network to raise money**
    *   Reality: Investors are "coin-operated lizard people" who care about making money above all else. They do not care about your pedigree if you are making something people want.
    *   Actionable Step: Founders must always talk to investors themselves. Never let an outsider or broker pitch your company for you.

*   **Myth 7: If investors reject your startup, it means it is a bad startup**
    *   Reality: Rejection is guaranteed. Even startups that reach multi-billion dollar valuations face immense rejection from investors early on.
    *   You do not need every investor to like what you are building; you just need a few of them to believe.
