# The Best Way To Launch Your Startup

[Video Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u36A-YTxiOw)

## Key Concepts & Advice

*   **Launch Continuously:** Stop treating a launch as a single, perfect moment. If you launch and no one cares, iterate and launch again.
*   **Launch ASAP:** Releasing an unpolished product early helps determine if you are actually solving a problem someone is willing to pay for or use. 
*   **Focus on Core Users:** It is better to make a few people really happy than a lot of people semi-happy. Find a core of users (even just 10) who absolutely love your product and expand from there.
*   **Press is Not a Silver Bullet:** Do not rely on press in the early stages. It is not a scalable way to get users, cannot be counted on for sustained growth, and will not get you to product-market fit.
*   **Always Be Launching:** Use every new product or feature release as an opportunity to launch again across all your channels.

## One-Sentence Pitch Strategy

*   **Lead with What, Not Why:** Start directly with your company name and what you do. Do not start with the backstory or the "why."
*   **Keep it Descriptive and Concise:** Clearly state the problem you are solving and who you are solving it for (e.g., "We built the first online marketplace that lets travelers book rooms with locals instead of hotels").
*   **No Marketing Speak:** Eliminate meaningless jargon, buzzwords, and rambling. The listener should instantly understand what they would have to build to reproduce your company.
*   **The "X for Y" Analogy:** Use this construction only if it meets three rules:
    1. X is a universally known household name.
    2. It is reasonably clear why Y would want X.
    3. Y represents a huge market.
    *   *Condition:* Always maintain a standard descriptive one-liner as a backup, in case the listener doesn't know company X.

## Types of Early Launches

*   **Silent Launch:** Publish a simple landing page containing your domain name, company name, short description, contact info, and a clear call-to-action (like an email waitlist).
*   **Friends and Family Launch:** Test your MVP and pitch on your inner circle to observe usage and get feedback. Do not stay in this phase long, as they are rarely your ideal target users.
*   **Strangers Launch:** Get direct feedback from hundreds of potential customers immediately to avoid spending months building the wrong solution.
*   **Online Communities Launch:** Launch in communities you are genuinely engaged with (e.g., Hacker News, Reddit). Be authentic and completely avoid overly promotional marketing language.
*   **Waitlist Launch:** Capture initial signups, but do not sit on the waitlist for too long; the longer you wait to onboard people, the harder it is to convert them.
*   **Pre-order Launch:** Viable for hardware or physical products via crowdfunding platforms, but approach with caution due to increased consumer skepticism.
*   **Owned Community Launch:** Start building your own community early, even just a simple email list of supporters, to engage them on an ongoing basis.
