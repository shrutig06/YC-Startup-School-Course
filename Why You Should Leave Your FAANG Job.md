# Why You Should Leave Your FAANG Job

Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sM2reZib2RY

Common Assumptions vs. Reality

* Founders mistake the employee path for the founder path, falling for campus recruiting cachet and brand rankings.
* Working on the hardest technical problems at scale is usually recruiter marketing; most engineers work on ad servers, buttons, or projects that get killed.
* Talk to people actually doing the job to learn the truth, not the recruiter whose compensation relies on getting you to sign.
* Big tech skills don't translate to startups; you rely on internal tooling (BigTable, BigQuery, custom infrastructure) that disappears when you start from scratch.
* Big companies teach processes you have to unlearn; a Google beta launch standard is 10x higher than what an MVP needs to be.
* The brand on your resume helps with VCs early on, but staying too long (e.g., eight years) erodes that advantage and turns into a negative signal.
* TechCrunch only covers successful raises; VCs do not hand out term sheets simply because you worked at Google or Facebook.

The Retention Trap

* Big tech companies deliberately warehouse talent to keep them away from competitors, often placing engineers on speculative moonshots that never ship.
* Retention is a gamified trap: initial equity vests over four years, and refreshers every six months create stacked loss aversion.
* Peer pressure drives lifestyle inflation (apartments, cars, vacations); if your unvested equity is 2x to 3x your savings, your brain convinces you that you can't afford to leave.

Signs It Is Time to Leave

* Waking up every day with fantasies of quitting, but staying only because of golden handcuffs.
* Suffering psychic scars and cynicism from work you feel is useless or unethical, which destroys the optimism required to be a founder.
* Realize that good programmers can always return to big tech later if a startup fails; the downside risk is low.

Actionable Strategy for Prospective Founders

* Enter with a concrete plan: know exactly what you are extracting (visa, savings, resume brand) and set a strict timeline for when to leave.
* Have your exit plan ready before facing a bonus or equity refresher, or the bonus will win.
* Keep personal burn low to avoid lifestyle creep and preserve financial freedom.
* Avoid toxic or cynical projects so you protect the optimism needed to build a company.
